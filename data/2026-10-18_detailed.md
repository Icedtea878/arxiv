原始摘要：Children are increasingly interacting with AI agents in learning and problem-solving contexts, where AI suggestions may appear fluent, helpful, and socially engaging. However, children may not always be able to judge whether such suggestions are accurate, incomplete, or misleading. This paper presents a game-based human-AI collaboration probe designed to examine how children verify AI suggestions during task-based interaction. The system embeds AI suggestions within a biological knowledge reasoning game and compares three Agent interaction styles: partner-like, tool-like, and dual-state Agent. Instead of treating trust as a static post-task attitude, this study focuses on children's situated verification behaviors, including acceptance, evidence checking, questioning, revision, and continued AI use. A preliminary evaluation with children aged 8-10 combined interaction logs, post-task ratings, and semi-structured interviews. Children generally viewed AI as useful but fallible, and their questioning behaviors varied in reasoning depth. The study contributes design insights for supporting children's critical, reflective, and evidence-based use of AI in collaborative tasks.

原始摘要：An agent that learns a user's preferences from their choices faces a cold start problem: before any choices are observed, it must rely solely on a prior. Informative priors are usually fitted to historical choice data, which new tasks and deployments lack. To address this, we present Agentic-Priors, which lets agentic systems use the implicit knowledge of large language models (LLMs) instead. Specifically, an LLM states a new user's trade-offs in interpretable terms, such as willingness to pay or value of time, which we map to a prior over multinomial logit coefficients. This prior is then refined by a Bayesian learner as choices are observed. Across seven choice datasets, Agentic-Priors improves cold start accuracy over an uninformed prior by 5-40 percentage points. It also outperforms AutoElicit, a state-of-the-art LLM prior-elicitation method, on three datasets and loses on one. It is up to 18 points more accurate than in-context learning, and never significantly worse. Against hierarchical Bayes models fitted to surveys of increasing size, our approach is worth roughly 6-40 surveyed users, in terms of log-loss, so its value is concentrated in a deployment's first users.

## 3. Explaining the Roles of Experts in Mixture-of-Experts LLMs for Personalization

**本篇未完成全文阅读总结。** 论文研究LLM个性化（用户偏好适配），方向上属于个体层面的用户模拟。通过MoE架构定位个性化专家的因果干预方法，可迁移用于理解模型如何编码用户特征。但核心贡献是模型可解释性技术，不是直接研究用户心理机制；迁移时需验证模型编码的用户特征与实际用户心理状态的一致性，不能直接假设技术层面的个性化等同于心理层面的用户模拟。

原始摘要：Personalization of Large Language Models (LLMs) is increasingly important for user-centric applications, yet prior methods mainly adapt dense LLMs, whose polysemantic neurons, each encoding multiple unrelated concepts, hinder localizing the components behind personalization, and thus understanding and controlling it. We propose to personalize Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLMs, which sparsely route each token to a few specialized experts, reducing polysemanticity and enabling such localization. We adapt three open-weight MoE LLMs on a large-scale personalization dataset and train crosscoders on paired activations of the personalized and non-personalized models to learn interpretable personalized latents, from which we identify the experts encoding distinct personalization features. The personalized MoE LLMs perform competitively with dense LLMs while activating only a fraction of their parameters, exhibit lower polysemanticity than dense LLMs with similar active parameters, and, within the same model family, yield personalized latents that are less shared with the non-personalized model. Causal interventions show that these latents suffice to recover the personalized model's behavior and that boosting their associated experts moves the non-personalized model toward it, revealing distinct routing paths for different personalization features.

## 4. Who May Release an Agent's Promise? Standing over Commitments in Multi-Party Chat

**本篇未完成全文阅读总结。** 该研究直接针对多智能体对话场景中的交互行为（谁有权限释放承诺），属于interaction方向的核心问题。研究了6个模型对多角色权限的处理能力，区分了两种错误类型，并验证了commitment ledger和policy能有效改善模型表现。虽然研究目标是提升assistant系统的可靠性，但明确提供了承诺管理机制的具体实现方式和实验验证，这些机制可迁移用于个体/代理的长期行为一致性和社会规范遵守的研究。

原始摘要：When an assistant serving a team makes a promise to Alice, who may cancel it? We call this standing: only the creditor (the person owed) or someone the creditor or operator policy authorizes may release a commitment, regardless of rank. In a controlled chat environment we vary who cancels and separate two errors: unauthorized release (obeying someone without standing) and override (executing a released promise). Across six models, smaller open ones override the creditor (Qwen3.5-9B: 69%); larger ones drop promises on a peer's direct word (Qwen3.8-27B: 89%; gpt-6.1-sol: 83%); nearly all act on a bystander's "go ahead after all". Oracles dissociate the errors: stating the rule or the creditor curbs unauthorized release but not override, which the latter even raises in three of four models; among them, only knowing whether the promise is still owed cuts override. Enabling thinking moves smaller models the way scale does. A self-kept commitment ledger stops override in four of five models; with a one-paragraph release policy it matches or beats the best oracle store, raising gpt-6-luna's strict accuracy from 49% (policy alone) to 83%. Code, prompts, outputs and labels are released.

This allows the use of such models in various contexts, including games and Tabletop Role-Playing Games (TTRPGs), such as Dungeons & Dragons. Overall, our framework appears to be a promising option for running entire TTRPG games.

原始摘要：The Perceived Empathy of Technology Scale (PETS) allows evaluation of empathic behavior in intelligent systems, a concept increasingly applied in human-AI interaction, particularly in emotional and mental health support applications. To support intercultural research and development, PETS is already available in multiple languages, as research suggests cultural and language-based differences in the expression and perception of empathy. Given the substantial Chinese-speaking population and the growing focus on cross-cultural HCI research, we present a validated translation of PETS into Putonghua (Mandarin) with Simplified Chinese. This validated Chinese version enables researchers and practitioners to assess empathy perceptions among Chinese-speaking users, supporting culturally appropriate empathic technology development.
