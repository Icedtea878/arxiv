原始摘要：Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) assumes that language models can reason over relations stated in their context rather than fall back on associations memorized during pretraining. We study this evidence-conditioned relational reasoning in isolation, with the facts supplied directly in the prompt rather than retrieved. \textsc{Rel-Bloom} is a controlled multilingual diagnostic of 450 multiple-choice questions built from 30 Wikidata relations; the same source relations are instantiated at five Bloom-inspired levels (Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate) and under three veracity conditions (Factual, Counterfactual, Neutralized). Across 43 models, six languages, and two multilingual settings (226,800 predictions), three findings stand out. First, accuracy drops sharply from Apply to Analyze in every evaluated open-weight model series, reaching 47.2 percentage points for the Large Granite bucket, but not for either Gemini model. Second, Evaluate items remain a bottleneck even for models near ceiling on the other task types: Gemini~3.1 Pro averages 99.5\% on the other four levels but 60.7\% on Evaluate. Third, item- and relation-level difficulty rankings are consistently preserved across languages, indicating a difficulty structure partly shared across languages within this benchmark. Across knowledge conditions, Counterfactual contexts are generally harder than Factual ones, while removing entity identities can improve higher-level performance in some model regimes. Greater model capability therefore does not by itself ensure reliable reasoning over supplied relational evidence. 原始摘要：PageRank is widely used to quantify the importance of nodes in directed networks. In applications such as recommendation systems, it is increasingly important that the PageRank distribution provides a fair representation of node exposure. This motivates the problem of making minimal changes to a graph so that the PageRank values of a given set of target nodes $T$ match a desired vector $\boldsymbol{t}$. In this paper, we study this problem under an edge-addition budget $B$, where exactly $B$ edges may be added to the graph. We show that deciding whether a multiset of exactly $B$ added edges can make the target nodes attain their desired absolute PageRank scores is NP-complete. After this, we define the $\ell_1$ \textsc{Target PageRank} problem in which we seek to minimize the $\ell_1$ distance between the target vector and resultant vector. We show that this optimization problem admits no polynomial-time multiplicative constant-factor approximation unless $P=NP$. Motivated by these hardness results, we propose two greedy heuristics for approaching the target PageRank vector. We additionally propose a Random Walk Hungarian (RWH) heuristic that addresses the addition of edges between dissimilar nodes by incorporating a penalty for such edges. Finally, we evaluate the proposed heuristics on several real-world and synthetic datasets and compare their performance in terms of PageRank accuracy, computational efficiency, and the relevance of the added edges.
